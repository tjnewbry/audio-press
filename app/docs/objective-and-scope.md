# Audio Press app: objective and scope

## Objective

A working prototype Android app (Android 13 and newer, API 33+) that uses the engine to turn a supported PDF into an audiobook and plays it.

## Features

The prototype has exactly these three features.

1. **Import PDF.** Pick a PDF with the system file picker. An import screen follows (see below), then the engine turns the chosen pages into a transcript, with progress shown while it works. Supported PDFs are text-based ones (Milestone Alpha). A scanned, password-protected, or damaged PDF shows a clear message, such as "scanned PDFs are not supported yet", and never crashes the app.
2. **Library.** A list of previously processed books. Selecting one opens it in the player.
3. **Player.**
   * **Live mode (the default):** reads the transcript aloud with Android's `TextToSpeech`, one paragraph per utterance, with play and pause.
   * **Bake:** a button that turns a book's transcript into audio files on the phone. Once a book is baked, the player plays those files.
   * **Live captions:** a scrolling transcript, like song lyrics in Spotify. The passage being read is highlighted and stays in view as the audio plays. In live mode, the current word is highlighted too, where the speech engine reports it. Captions do not highlight anything on the PDF pages; that needs later engine milestones.
   * **Tap a line to jump:** tapping a passage in the captions moves playback there.
   * **Timestamp:** elapsed and total time. In live mode these are estimates (from word count and speaking rate, refined as it plays); for a baked book they are the real audio durations.
   * **Saved progress:** the position is saved automatically, and reopening a book resumes where it left off, in either mode.

**Player screen (redesign after A6):** the bake and speech messages are at the top, the book text is in a bounded, scrollable box in the middle, and the controls are at the bottom: the play and pause icon (no words) level with the centre line of a seeker bar. The played position advances smoothly (about every 150 ms: the clip position for baked audio, an interpolation between the word reports of the speech engine for live speech). The start and end of the book sit one end-cap radius in from the ends of the track, and the thumb, the fills, the arrow (white with a dark outline) and touches all use that one scale. Progress bars have no end dot. A reading speed control (0.5x to 2x in steps of 0.05 (without 0.95x and 1.05x), saved per book in `speed.txt`) sits at the top right: a large label such as `1.15x`, which opens a popup with the speed in two decimals in a fixed-width monospace box and a vertical slider, and stays open until a tap outside it. It sets the baked audio's playback speed (pitch kept; the bake is never redone) and the live voice's speech rate at once, and leaves the bar, the buffer, the dots and the time text on the book's 1x timeline. The time text follows the finger while the bar is dragged, "Return to player position" jumps with no animation, the voice screen opens on Medium, else Lite, else Heavy (the downloaded one), and in live-speech mode the block dots are drawn over the buffer. Seeking is to the exact millisecond in baked audio (live speech snaps to the line, as does the unbaked part of a book being baked, which is read live) and never starts playback. The block dots sit under the buffer. The text under the bar is worked out from the mode and bake state, so it is right after a toggle or a bake ending. The bar has a coloured fill for the position read, a grey fill for how much is baked, and tap or drag to seek. All of it, and the scroll arrow, use one time scale: the exact baked time plus an estimate (170 words per minute, or the baked speed once enough is baked) for what is not baked. The grey fill grows as clips finish, also while the book is open during a bake, and is absent for live speech with nothing baked. When the user scrolls the box by hand, a downward arrow on the bar shows where the box is (the top edge of the text slides to the bottom edge towards the end of the book, so the arrow reaches both ends) and a "Return to player position" button appears; tapping it, a line, or the bar locks the box to the playing line again.

**Import screen (added after A6):** after the file picker the PDF is copied into the app's cache (`import-staging`, deleted on Import, Cancel, or the next launch) and opened once to read its length and title. The screen has Cancel on the left, the book's name in the middle (tap to rename) and Import on the right; "PDF length: N pages."; "Selected range: A - B" as two number boxes (digits only, at least 1, at most N as typed; a start after the end, or an end before the start, is corrected when the box is left, and the range in use is never impossible); and a lazily drawn list of page previews (Android's `PdfRenderer`, at most 360 px wide, a 12 MB cache, drawn only when visible; a page that cannot be drawn, or a PDF the renderer cannot open, shows a blank page with its number). Pages outside the range are dimmed. A red dash with a down arrow marks the top of the first imported page and one with an up arrow the bottom of the last, in the left margin; a grey dash at every other page boundary opens a "Mark as" popup offering only valid choices (before the start marker only "start", after the end marker only "end", between them both; tapping outside cancels). A scroll thumb on the right follows the scroll position and can be dragged; while held, a bubble to its left names the page at the thumb's height. The name defaults to the PDF's title (else the file name), gets " (pp. A-B)" ("(p. A)" for one page) while the range is not the whole PDF and the user has not typed a name, and a typed name is never touched. Import as the first touch of the screen goes ahead at once; after any other touch, if the user did not rename, a dialog asks "Import as '...'?" (Import, Rename, Cancel). The book's page count is the number of pages in the range. Encrypted and damaged PDFs show the usual message in the library instead of the screen. The rules are in `ImportRange.kt` (unit-tested); the screen is in `ui/pdfimport/`, the renderer `PdfPreviews.kt`.

**Speed control and Info (added after the import screen):** the closed speed label sits in a box exactly as wide as `0.85x` (an invisible copy sets the size, the real label is right-justified over it and takes no part in the size, so a label that is wider in some font spills out to the left on one line and nothing moves) and at least 48 dp tall, so its digits are level with the middle of the Bake button beside it. The open number is drawn in the system monospace font, in the same kind of box; the popup is placed so the open number's right edge and top are those of the closed number (the panel's padding reaches past them), and the closed number is not drawn while it is open. Both use tabular figures (`tnum`) where the font has them. While the slider moves, baked audio changes speed at each step; live speech changes speed, and the speed is saved, when the finger lifts. The player screen keeps one layout from its first frame: nothing shows while the speech engine starts (a play tapped then waits for it), and only a speech engine that cannot be used shows a message. No font is bundled: tjnewbry chose that, so on a phone whose font theme ignores the monospace request (their Moto) the number shows in the phone's own font, and the box, sized by an invisible `0.85x`, still fits every label; a thin dash across the slider marks 1x. 0.95x and 1.05x are not offered (29 speeds; saved values snap to 1x). The library row's three-dots menu has Info (between Rename and Deletion): source file, source length, imported range, bake data and total size, in decimal units (`BookInfo.kt`). `book.properties` records the source (`sourceFile`, `sourcePages`, `sourceFirst`, `sourceLast`); a book imported earlier shows "Unknown" and its own page count with range 1 to that count; a missing or damaged record never hides a book.

**Not in the prototype:** skip buttons, chapter navigation, speed or voice settings, playback with the screen off, scanned PDFs, and highlighting words on the PDF pages.

**Deleting (added after A6):** each library row has a three-dots menu (Export baked audio, which is grey until the book is fully baked (a tap then says "Must bake audio first.") and otherwise opens the save dialog with the title plus .m4a, encodes the clips into one AAC .m4a file in a temp file, copies it to the chosen place, and ends with a success dialog giving the file size and what the book still takes in the app; it runs in a foreground service with a silent ongoing notification and Cancel, and a progress dialog; Rename, which trims the title and ignores a blank or unchanged one; and Deletion). Deletion opens a dialog with Delete all (the book and its baked audio), Delete bake (only the baked audio and saved voice; the book stays) and Cancel. A running download or bake of that book is cancelled and waited for first. Downloaded voice models are shared and never deleted. Import keeps no copy of the PDF (the temporary copy is deleted when the import ends), so there is nothing else to remove.

## Decisions

* **The transcript is made at import,** in both modes. Baking only adds audio; it never re-reads the PDF.
* **The engine is used as a library,** through [engine/docs/api.md](../../engine/docs/api.md). PDFBox does not run on Android, so the app needs its own PDF reader.
* **The Android PDF reader** lives in `app/pdf-source/`, an Android library module that implements the engine's `TextSource`. The engine owner builds and owns it; the rest of `app/` belongs to the app developer.
* **Processing runs on the phone,** with PdfBox-Android (Apache 2.0) reading the PDFs. Decided 2026-10-31 after the PDF-reader research; no server is needed.
* **Baked audio is large:** uncompressed speech is measured at about 3 MB per minute (about 175 MB per hour). Compressing it is a later improvement.
* **Baking uses Kokoro v1.0 voices (sprint A6).** Live playback stays on the phone's own speech engine. Tapping Bake opens a voice screen with three model qualities (Lite = int8, Medium = a mixed uint8/int8 model (the release names the file fp16), Heavy = fp32; about 137, 222, and 370 MB), downloaded on demand, never shipped in the APK, and 28 English voices with short samples (shipped as Ogg Opus in the app, about 1.3 MB) to listen to first. Switching quality tabs deselects the voice and keeps the list position. The button under the list always keeps room for two lines (a download offer takes two), so the list never changes size. Removing downloaded models is not in A6. 
* **A download or bake keeps running in the background (sprint A6).** It runs in a foreground service of type `dataSync` with a notification ("Downloading Lite...", "Baking audio using Santa...", progress, Cancel), and the CPU is held awake, so a long bake continues with the screen off or another app in front. Android 13 asks for the notification permission when the voice screen opens, and the bake runs either way. From Android 15 a `dataSync` service may run only about 6 hours in 24, so a long bake is normally resumed: when the limit comes the bake stops cleanly and keeps its clips, and the book offers "Resume baking" (as it does after the app is killed). The app needs the `INTERNET` permission for the model download and nothing else uses the network.

## Definition of done

1. The app builds from a clean checkout with its own Gradle wrapper, its tests pass, and the engine still builds on its own.
2. It runs on the Android 13 emulator and on the owner's phone.
3. Importing a text-based PDF adds a book to the library. Scanned, password-protected, and damaged PDFs show clear messages.
4. Live mode reads the book in order, with captions that follow along, a timestamp, and tap-a-line to jump.
5. Baking produces audio, and a baked book plays with captions and real timestamps.
6. Progress survives closing and reopening the app, in both modes.
7. No copyrighted PDFs are committed, and the docs match the app.
8. The owner has checked it on their phone with real books and verified the prototype.
